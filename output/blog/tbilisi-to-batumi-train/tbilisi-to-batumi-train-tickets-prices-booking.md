# Tbilisi to Batumi Train: Tickets, Prices & Booking

The Tbilisi to Batumi train is a practical way to connect Georgia’s capital with its Black Sea coast without driving across the country. Booking it takes a little planning: tickets have a limited advance-sales window, families need seats together, and the arrival station is separate from Batumi’s central hotel areas. This guide explains how to choose a departure, budget for the journey, handle luggage and build the transfer into a holiday in Georgia, the country.

## Tbilisi to Batumi train: the quick answer

The current operator timetable lists journeys of approximately four hours. That is the time on the train; keep additional space in your day for reaching the station, boarding and continuing to your hotel.

- Route: Tbilisi Central to Batumi Central.
- Journey time: approximately four hours on the services currently listed by Georgian Railway.
- Advertised starting fare: TKT.GE lists Batumi tickets from 35 GEL; check your date, class and checkout fees.
- Advance booking: the railway’s domestic online-sales terms specify up to 20 calendar days, with exceptions possible.
- Best planning approach: reserve an available departure before fixing activities around it.

Information was checked on 22 September 2026. Prices and operating times can change; the departure shown on your purchased ticket is the one to follow.

This article covers the journey itself. For deciding whether the coast belongs in your holiday, read our [Batumi guide](/blog/batumi-guide-is-it-worth-adding-to-your-georgia-itinerary).

### Which departure should you choose?

For a short Batumi stay, a morning departure gives you more useful time after arrival. You can drop bags, eat lunch and leave your first afternoon flexible rather than racing to a timed attraction.

The later departure can suit travelers who want another morning in Tbilisi. Plan an easy lunch, collect stored luggage and leave a generous station buffer. Confirm late check-in with your Batumi accommodation before choosing an evening arrival.

For a family, the easiest departure may simply be the one that preserves normal meals and avoids waking children unusually early. An extra relaxed hour can matter more than squeezing in one more sight.

## Ticket prices and travel classes

[TKT.GE’s railway booking page](https://tkt.ge/en/railway) currently advertises Batumi fares from 35 GEL. Treat that as a starting point: the class, available service and added fees determine the amount you actually pay.

For two adults, two one-way fares at that advertised minimum would total 70 GEL before additional fees. A return journey doubles the rail component, and station transfers sit outside it. This is a worked budget example, not a quote.

Compare the full journey cost with your group size in mind. Four separate rail tickets plus two transfers are a different calculation from one solo traveler carrying a backpack.

### Second class, first class or business?

Second class is the sensible starting point if your priority is keeping transport spending down. First class is worth comparing when extra legroom matters; business class is the higher-comfort option.

Check the seat map and the price difference on your departure. A family may get more value from adjacent standard seats than from upgrading into a carriage with scattered availability.

Do not assume a higher class changes the journey time. Choose it for the space or comfort offered, and check any advertised inclusions before paying. For wider accommodation and food costs, our [Georgia trip budget guide](/blog/how-much-does-a-trip-to-georgia-cost-budget-breakdown) helps put the ticket price in context.

## How to book Tbilisi to Batumi train tickets

Start with the railway’s own booking website, gr.com.ge, or a railway ticketing partner such as TKT.GE. Use the platform’s English interface if that makes passenger details and refund conditions easier to understand.

The [current Georgian Railway booking terms](https://gr.com.ge/en/info-content) state a domestic advance-sales window of up to 20 calendar days, except in exceptional cases. A date outside that window may simply be unreleased.

### A booking checklist

1. Enter Tbilisi as the departure and Batumi as the destination.
2. Select your actual date and the correct adult and child categories.
3. Compare departure and arrival times before choosing a class.
4. Check adjacent-seat availability if you need to sit together.
5. Enter identity details carefully, following the selected platform’s instructions.
6. Review the final price and cancellation terms, then save the issued ticket offline.

Keep the booking confirmation somewhere every traveling adult can access. A screenshot or downloaded ticket is useful when a phone has weak reception, but bring the required original identity document too.

Before leaving the payment page, check that you have an issued ticket showing the correct route and date. A bank notification alone is less useful than the actual travel document. If payment appears to have succeeded but no ticket arrives, check your account and contact the seller before paying again.

### No trains showing: unreleased or sold out?

An empty search result is not enough to tell the difference. First check whether the date falls within the sales window, then check nearby days and the other departures.

If your trip is approaching and available seats are limited, decide which constraint is flexible: departure time, class or travel date. Families should prioritize a workable seating arrangement rather than buying isolated seats and hoping other passengers will move.

If seats are genuinely sold out, compare a scheduled coach or a private transfer. Ask a transfer provider for the total group price, luggage capacity, child-seat arrangements and pickup details. Confirm a replacement before abandoning your existing plan.

## Boarding in Tbilisi and arriving in Batumi

### Tbilisi Central: allow time to find your train

The Batumi service departs from Tbilisi Central Railway Station at Station Square. It is not an airport departure, and it should not be confused with the city’s intercity bus terminals.

Our recommendation is to arrive around 30–45 minutes before departure, especially on your first visit or with children and large bags. This is a practical buffer, not a stated boarding deadline.

Have your ticket and passport accessible before reaching the carriage. Put documents in your day bag rather than in a suitcase that you will immediately store. Check the carriage and seat numbers before settling in.

If you arrive in Georgia by air that morning, leave a substantial connection margin for immigration, baggage collection and road traffic. A delayed flight can turn a cheap advance train ticket into an awkward first day. An overnight in Tbilisi is often the calmer choice.

### Batumi Central: plan the final transfer

Batumi Central is outside the main central sightseeing and hotel areas. Plan onward transport to your exact accommodation rather than assuming that arrival means a short walk to your room.

Save the hotel’s map pin and check the entrance instructions before leaving Tbilisi. This is particularly useful for apartment stays where the building name, reception desk and actual entrance may differ.

For an evening arrival, agree on late check-in and keep dinner simple. With a family or several suitcases, arranging the pickup in advance can make the last part of the day easier.

Avoid scheduling a paid excursion immediately after arrival. A relaxed first evening gives you room for ordinary delays, checking in and finding your bearings.

## Luggage, food and traveling with children

Pack for a seated rail journey with transfers at both ends. Even if your luggage is within the permitted allowance, you still need to move it through the station and store it without blocking circulation.

### Suitcases and onboard essentials

The railway’s published allowance is up to 36 kg of hand luggage with combined length, width and height no greater than 180 cm. Check oversized items directly before travel; do not rely on anecdotes about what another passenger carried.

Keep these items in a smaller bag:

- Tickets, passports and accommodation details.
- Drinking water and an easy meal or snacks.
- Any medication you need during the journey.
- A light layer, headphones and a charged power bank.
- Offline entertainment and downloaded maps for arrival.

Treat food availability and internet access as bonuses rather than the foundation of your plan. Bringing familiar food is especially useful with children, dietary requirements or a tight morning departure.

Ask the attendant where bulky bags belong. Keep doorways and aisles clear, and avoid packing something essential at the bottom of a suitcase you cannot easily reach.

### Child tickets and seats together

The railway allows one child under five to travel free with an adult if the child does not occupy a separate seat. A child who needs a seat requires the appropriate ticket; children from age five to under ten use child fares, while adult rules apply from ten. Enter the child’s details and carry the required identification.

For a multi-hour journey, consider buying a seat for a younger child even when sharing is permitted. Being able to eat, nap or play beside you may justify the cost.

Before paying, check the actual seating arrangement. Two adults separated from their children is a poor trade for a slightly cheaper departure. Pack a small activity bag so you do not need to unpack the main luggage during the ride.

If you need wheelchair assistance, contact Georgian Railway in advance; its terms request at least 48 hours’ notice. Discuss the station, boarding and arrival assistance you need rather than assuming that an accessible carriage solves every part of the journey.

## Train, coach or private driver: which fits your route?

Choose the train when you want a straightforward city-to-city transfer at a known departure time. It works particularly well when your main sightseeing happens before and after the journey.

A coach is a useful alternative when rail tickets do not match your dates. Compare its actual terminals, luggage policy and arrival time; the cheapest ticket is less attractive if it leaves you with an inconvenient late transfer.

A private driver is worth considering when you want hotel-to-hotel transport, flexible breaks or sightseeing stops along the way. Get a written total and confirm what waiting time and detours are included.

Self-driving makes more sense as part of a wider road trip than as an automatic choice for this single transfer. Think about parking, rental return arrangements and whether you actually need a vehicle during your Batumi stay.

The key comparison is door to door. Add station or terminal transfers and waiting time to the advertised journey, then choose the option that fits your group’s pace.

## Fit the train into your Georgia itinerary

Treat the transfer as a distinct part of your trip, with light activities on arrival. A four-hour rail journey still occupies a meaningful portion of the day once you add checkout and station transfers.

A manageable outline is two or three nights in Tbilisi, a morning train west, then at least two nights in Batumi. This is a planning suggestion: your interests and flight route should determine the final balance.

Use your Tbilisi days for the capital and any eastern-Georgia excursions before moving to the coast. If you want a guided introduction, TripMate’s [private half-day Tbilisi city tour](/tours/tbilisi-city-tour-old-town-narikala-and-panoramic-views) fits naturally on the day before your train. It is a separate sightseeing booking, not part of the rail ticket.

A Kutaisi stop needs its own transport plan. Do not treat a through ticket as permission to leave the train, spend a night elsewhere and resume the next day. Arrange the separate legs and check the exact stations.

For your return, protect the international flight connection. Returning to Tbilisi the day before an important flight leaves more room for disruption than making the entire cross-country journey on departure day.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does the Tbilisi to Batumi train take?

The live operator timetable checked on 22 September 2026 lists approximately four hours. Add station transfers and boarding time, and confirm the duration on your selected departure.

### How much are Tbilisi to Batumi train tickets?

TKT.GE advertises fares from 35 GEL. Your final total depends on the available class and added fees; check the checkout price for your actual date.

### How far in advance can I book?

Georgian Railway’s domestic online-sales terms specify up to 20 calendar days, with possible exceptions. If a later date is missing, check again when it enters the sales window.

### Can I bring a large suitcase?

Yes, provided it meets the railway’s allowance: up to 36 kg and 180 cm in combined dimensions. Confirm unusual or oversized luggage in advance and keep circulation areas clear.

### Is a Batumi day trip from Tbilisi realistic by train?

We recommend staying overnight. Even with the shorter current journey, two rail legs plus station transfers consume much of a day, leaving limited time to enjoy the coast.
