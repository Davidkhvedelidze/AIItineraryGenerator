Mother of Georgia is the silver figure above Old Tbilisi, the historic heart of Georgia’s capital. Visiting Kartlis Deda works best as a short hilltop outing: ride up from Rike Park, walk to the statue, then decide whether to return by cable car or continue downhill on foot. This guide separates the monument from the transport, explains the symbolism and helps you build a manageable route around it.

## Mother of Georgia at a glance

- Location: Sololaki Hill, above Old Tbilisi.
- Monument: a 20-metre figure of a woman holding a bowl and a sword.
- Main approach: Rike–Narikala cable car, followed by a short walk.
- Alternative: an uphill walk using the Betlemi Street stairway.
- Suggested time: allow 60–90 minutes for a relaxed cable-car outing, longer if queues build.
- Best fit: first-time visitors, city photography and a short introduction to Georgian cultural symbolism.

Treat these visit durations as planning allowances, not measured journey times or a guarantee of queue length. The statue itself does not require a long visit, but getting everyone to the station, paying and stopping for photographs can take longer than the ride.

## What does the Mother of Georgia statue mean?

The bowl in her left hand represents hospitality toward friends; the sword in her right represents defending the country against enemies. It is a public symbol, not a portrait of a particular queen.

Sculptor Elguja Amashukeli created the monument for Tbilisi’s 1,500th anniversary in 1958. The original wooden work was covered in aluminium in 1963; a replacement followed in 1997. The Georgian National Tourism Administration explains this history in its [Kartlis Deda overview](https://georgia.travel/kartlis-deda).

Knowing the meaning before you arrive makes the stop more interesting, especially with children. Ask them to look for the two objects from a safe place on the path. You do not need to turn the visit into a lengthy history lesson: the contrast between welcoming a guest and protecting a home is easy to discuss while looking over the city.

Expect an outdoor monument and a viewpoint rather than an indoor attraction. Plan your time around the approach, the view and the surrounding walk.

## How to get to Mother of Georgia

### Cable car from Rike Park

For most first-time visitors, the simplest starting point is the lower Rike–Narikala station in Rike Park. This is the route you want for Mother of Georgia; a ride toward Mtatsminda or Turtle Lake takes you somewhere else.

The tourism board describes the ride as roughly two minutes, with about five minutes of walking from the upper station to the statue. Allow extra time for boarding, photographs and the pace of your group. The quoted ride time does not include the queue.

At the upper station, follow the main route toward Kartlis Deda. Pause away from the station exit before checking your map so that other passengers can pass. If you plan to walk down, identify your descent before leaving the ridge rather than following a convenient-looking staircase without checking where it leads.

### Walking up from Old Tbilisi

The Betlemi Street stairway is the main walking alternative identified by the tourism board. Choose it if you enjoy exploring on foot and everyone in your group is comfortable with an uphill approach.

Save the statue and your starting point on a map before setting off. A short distance on the screen can conceal a substantial change in elevation. Wear shoes with grip and leave room in your schedule for breaks; this is not the walk to squeeze between two fixed reservations.

Walking up also changes the shape of your outing. You can explore the Old Town first, climb when you are ready and use the cable car for the descent if it is running. Conversely, riding up and walking down saves the uphill effort but still involves slopes and potentially stairs. Downhill does not automatically mean easy for someone with knee problems.

### Arriving with a driver

Tell your driver whether you want the Rike cable-car station or a walking-route starting point. Simply sending a monument pin may not communicate the best vehicle drop-off.

For a private visit, agree how much walking is comfortable and where you will meet afterward. A driver can simplify the journeys between neighbourhoods, but should not be treated as a guarantee of vehicle access to the statue itself. Keep the return meeting point somewhere both you and the driver can identify.

## Tickets, cable-car fares and opening times

The outdoor statue is free to see. Transport and any separate attractions you add are different expenses.

The [Tbilisi Transport Company tariff](https://ttc.com.ge/en/tariff/10), checked on 5 November 2026, lists the Rike–Narikala ropeway at 2.50 GEL one way. Riding up and down therefore costs 5 GEL per paying passenger, before any card purchase. The same tariff lists a 2 GEL purchase price for a Metromoney card.

For a simple adult example, two return journeys total 10 GEL in ride fares. If each adult buys a new 2 GEL card, that example totals 14 GEL. Existing cards, eligibility for concessions and the payment method you use can change what you need to buy; ask at the station rather than assuming a child travels free.

Keep the cable-car budget separate from your ordinary metro or bus pass. Our [Tbilisi public transport guide](/blog/tbilisi-public-transport-metro-bus-payment-guide) explains the difference between stored credit and travel subscriptions.

For hours, the tourism board gives a general 10:00–22:00 window, with possible longer seasonal operation. Confirm the day’s service and last descent with station staff, particularly if you intend to stay for evening views. A published visitor timetable is not a live guarantee against maintenance, weather disruption or an operational change.

Have your payment method ready before joining the boarding line. If you need to purchase or top up a card, resolve that first. Do not assume a bank card accepted elsewhere in Tbilisi will necessarily work at this gate.

## How much time should you allow?

A 60–90-minute slot is a useful starting point for a cable-car visit with time on the ridge. Add a buffer when your day includes children, a large group or a reservation afterward. If the queue is long, decide whether the view is worth the wait before committing everyone to the line.

For a slower Old Town outing, reserve half a day and include a meal. This gives you room to linger without making the statue itself sound like a half-day attraction.

Before going up, agree on the return plan. Groups often lose time because one person expects to descend by cable car while another wants a long walk through a different neighbourhood. Choose a default and leave optional extensions genuinely optional.

## A relaxed route around the statue

### Start with Rike Park and the river

Meet in the Rike area and give yourself a little time beside the river before boarding. Buy water beforehand and handle practical stops before heading up. This is also a sensible point to check whether everybody wants to ride or whether someone prefers to remain at ground level.

Keep your initial plan compact. Adding several sights before the cable car can mean arriving tired at the only uphill walking section of the day.

### Ride up, visit Kartlis Deda and enjoy the view

After the ride, walk to the statue and allow time to look at both the monument and the city. Give each person a chance to take photographs without holding up the path.

Narikala is nearby, but a visit to Mother of Georgia does not confirm access to every part of the fortress. Check local signs and barriers before adding it. Do not build a fixed route around entering fortress areas whose current access you have not confirmed.

### Choose one extension

After the hilltop visit, choose either more Old Town walking, a meal or a bath appointment. You do not need to combine all three.

The National Botanical Garden is another possible addition, but treat it as a separate visit with its own admission, entrances and closing time. Do not assume the garden is an always-open shortcut downhill.

If you prefer a bath afterward, book with enough space between activities to absorb a transport delay. Our [Tbilisi sulfur baths guide](/blog/tbilisi-sulfur-baths-prices-booking-etiquette) covers room selection and booking questions. An appointment should shape your descent time rather than force you to rush down unfamiliar steps.

## Visiting with children or limited mobility

For families, a short cable-car outing may be more manageable than a long stair climb. Explain the return plan to children before setting off, carry water and keep younger children close on the ridge.

Avoid promising a completely step-free experience based on the cabin alone. Boarding arrangements, the station exit and the onward path all matter. If wheelchair access is essential, ask the operator about current arrangements and discuss the full route with your guide before booking.

With a stroller, decide whether you are prepared to turn back if the chosen path becomes awkward. A return cable-car journey can be a simpler default than committing to an unfamiliar downhill route. ## When to visit and how to photograph it

Choose the clearest part of your day if the panorama is your priority. In warm weather, avoid combining a demanding climb with the hottest hours. In cooler months, bring an extra layer for standing still outdoors and make the descent before dark if you are unfamiliar with the route.

You have two different photographic subjects: the statue and the city below. A close viewpoint emphasizes the sculpture’s scale and details; stepping back along a permitted path can give you a more complete composition. Do not expect every city panorama to include the front of the statue as well.

Take photographs from established public paths and keep your feet inside safe boundaries. If the light is poor, a shorter visit can still be worthwhile for understanding the landmark. There is no need to wait indefinitely for a particular sunset shot.

## Visiting independently or with a private tour

You can visit independently if you are comfortable navigating the station and walking route. A guide is useful when you want historical context, help managing the group’s pace or a broader city itinerary.

For a relaxed visit, ask for fewer stops with enough time at each. The useful outcome is a city day everyone enjoys, with Mother of Georgia as a memorable part of it.
