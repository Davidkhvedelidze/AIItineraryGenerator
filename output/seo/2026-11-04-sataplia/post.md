# Sataplia Nature Reserve: Dinosaur Tracks & Cave Guide

Sataplia Nature Reserve combines dinosaur footprints, a cave and a forest walk close to Kutaisi, in Georgia's Imereti region. It is a good option when your group wants several different things to look at without building a whole day around a long drive. This guide explains the current ticket information, what the visit involves and how to choose between Sataplia and another cave or canyon outing.

## Why choose Sataplia?

Sataplia's strength is variety. You can make the preserved tracks the focus, enjoy the underground scenery, or treat the visit as a walk with interesting stops. That makes it particularly useful for a mixed-age family whose members would not all choose the same activity.

Our recommendation is to choose Sataplia when the footprints and the forest interest you as much as the cave. If you are looking mainly for a substantial underground experience, compare it with Prometheus before buying tickets for both. Two attractions with caves do not automatically give you two equally different days out.

It is also a sensible way to give children some ownership of a Georgia itinerary. Let them choose a question to ask about the tracks or a detail to look for underground. A visit built around curiosity is more engaging than announcing another stop where everyone must follow the adults and pose for photographs.

The location works best as part of a Kutaisi stay. Start with our [Kutaisi travel guide](/blog/kutaisi-travel-guide-canyons-caves-and-georgias-second-city) if you have not yet decided how to divide your time between the city and the surrounding countryside.

## The dinosaur footprints: what you are coming to see

The main distinction is between preserved evidence and visitor displays. The footprints are the reason many travellers seek out Sataplia; the dinosaur models are separate exhibits, rather than the fossil tracks themselves.

The [official Sataplia route description](https://www.nationalparks.ge/en/site/sataplia/tripsInner/54) includes a building protecting dinosaur tracks, an exhibition hall, a cave, Colchic forest and a glass viewpoint. Dinosaur models also appear along the route. Use that list to set expectations: this is a protected natural site with visitor facilities, not a large theme park.

At the tracks, give yourself time to listen to the explanation before moving on. Ask the guide how a footprint differs from the surrounding rock and what the arrangement of tracks can tell us. For children, these concrete questions are easier to follow than a stream of unfamiliar prehistoric names.

Avoid promising that every child will immediately recognise every print. Looking at traces in rock takes more attention than looking at a model dinosaur. Comparing the two can become part of the fun: one is evidence left behind, while the other helps visitors imagine an animal.

Photographs are useful reminders, but keep the protected surfaces and barriers intact. Do not try to make the marks clearer by touching, wetting or tracing them. Let the guide point out the features from the permitted viewing position.

## Inside Sataplia Cave

The cave gives the outing a different pace after the tracks and the forest. Expect a managed visitor experience, with a route through rock formations, rather than a free-ranging caving expedition.

[Georgia's national tourism guide](https://georgia.travel/caving-in-sataplia-cave) describes a visitor cave section of around 300 metres and an average interior temperature of 14°C. That underground distance is not the length of the whole reserve visit. You still need time for the other attractions and the walking between them.

Bring a light layer even on a warm day. Shoes with grip are more useful than footwear chosen only for photographs, and a small bag leaves your hands available when moving through the route. Keep a jacket accessible instead of burying it under luggage in the vehicle.

Children who dislike enclosed spaces may need a simple explanation before entering. Tell them they will follow a visitor route with a guide and stay with the group. If anyone is uncomfortable, ask staff about the practical options before committing to the underground section.

For visitors with mobility needs, contact the reserve about the exact route. A short cave does not establish that there are no steps, gradients or awkward surfaces. Describe the assistance you require rather than asking only whether the attraction is “easy.”

## Forest paths and the glass viewpoint

The official route is listed as an easy, roughly two-kilometre walk. Its forest sections are part of the experience, not simply the distance you must cover between paid attractions. Leave enough time to notice the woodland instead of treating every stretch outside as a transfer.

The glass viewpoint is an additional attraction, but your enjoyment should not depend entirely on stepping onto it. People who dislike heights can make the tracks, cave and forest their priorities. Nobody needs to complete every feature for the visit to count.

The [park's visitor rules](https://nationalparks.ge/en/site/sataplia/guide) prohibit access to the panoramic platform in rain or snow. They also require visitors to stay on marked paths, prohibit pets and restrict collecting natural materials. Check the day's arrangements before buying admission if the viewpoint is your main reason for coming.

For a family, decide in advance how to handle different comfort levels. One adult can stay with a child who does not want to approach the glass, while others follow staff instructions. A calm alternative works better than turning the platform into a test of bravery.

Keep photographs brief at narrow places and let other visitors pass. The best memory of the viewpoint may be the wider landscape, not a carefully staged image of your feet.

## Sataplia tickets and opening hours

The [Agency of Protected Areas tariff](https://www.apa.ge/en/eco-tourism/servisebi-da-tarifebi/sataflia), checked on 4 November 2026, lists these admission rates:

- Foreign adults: 30 GEL.
- Georgian citizens and residents: 15 GEL.
- Ages 6–18 on the English-language listing: 5.50 GEL.
- Children under six: free.

Guide service is included, with Georgian, English and Russian listed. Bring identification for reduced rates and confirm a child's applicable category before payment.

Two foreign adults should therefore allow 60 GEL for admission, excluding transport and meals. Do the calculation for your actual party before comparing a driver quote with an excursion package. Ask whether the package includes entrance tickets, and whether the guide mentioned is the reserve guide or someone accompanying you throughout the journey.

For November planning, the National Parks overview lists daily autumn and winter hours of 10:00–17:00. APA also lists daily opening, but older visitor information still excludes Mondays. Confirm a Monday visit and the final guided entry directly; visitor services publishes +995 595 08 60 59.

We recommend a morning arrival or an early-afternoon visit with plenty of margin. A closing time does not tell you when the last complete route begins. Arriving shortly before closing is especially unhelpful if the tracks, cave and viewpoint all matter to your group.

Treat old screenshots of tickets as historical information. Ask for the current total at the desk, and keep transport money separate from your admission budget.

## Getting to Sataplia from Kutaisi

The visitor centre is in Banoja, in Tskaltubo municipality. Save the Sataplia visitor-centre destination rather than navigating simply to “Tskaltubo,” which also brings up the spa town and other attractions.

A taxi or prearranged driver is the most straightforward option for a party that wants one reserve visit and a return to Kutaisi. Ask for a complete quote with waiting time and the return destination included. If you want to add another attraction, mention it before agreeing on the price.

For planning, allow roughly half a day away from your accommodation, including the journey and an unhurried visit. Check a current road estimate from your own pickup point: being “near Kutaisi” does not mean every hotel or airport transfer starts in the same place.

If you prefer local transport, ask your accommodation or a local information desk to confirm both the outward connection and the final journey to the entrance. Arrange the return before leaving. We have not verified a dependable current direct-bus timetable, so do not build a tight itinerary around an assumed departure.

Self-driving is useful if you already have a rental car for a wider route. Save the location, check the day's weather and leave enough time to return before your next fixed commitment. There is little benefit in hiring a car solely to avoid asking a driver about a short return trip.

## A half-day plan that leaves room to enjoy the reserve

Allow approximately two to three hours at Sataplia as our planning recommendation, especially with children. This includes stops, explanations and a comfortable walking pace; it is not a guaranteed guided-tour duration.

A practical sequence is to confirm the day's route at the entrance, follow the staff's order through the attractions and leave lunch until after the visit. Bring drinking water and make any necessary comfort stops before joining the group.

Build the afternoon around how everyone feels afterwards:

- If the family is satisfied, return to Kutaisi for lunch and a relaxed city walk.
- If you want a different local experience, consider a permitted public-area visit in Tskaltubo.
- If caves are a strong shared interest, consider Prometheus only after checking its entry arrangements and allowing a proper break.

Our [Tskaltubo guide](/blog/tskaltubo-abandoned-sanatoriums-complete-guide) provides context for the resort town. Do not treat abandoned buildings as freely accessible sightseeing stops; decide on appropriate public areas before extending your route.

Avoid adding every cave and canyon in western Georgia to the same day. The issue is not just driving time: each ticket desk, group departure, walking circuit and meal needs its own allowance. Choose a second stop because it interests you, not because a map makes it look close.

## Sataplia or Prometheus Cave?

Choose Sataplia for a varied outing centred on dinosaur tracks, forest and an underground section. Choose Prometheus when your main interest is spending more of the visit in a larger cave setting. Our [Prometheus Cave guide](/blog/prometheus-cave-tickets-boat-visit-guide) explains its separate boat option and the practical costs.

For a family with one spare half-day, we would usually select the attraction that matches the children's interests most clearly. Dinosaur enthusiasm is a good reason to choose Sataplia. A group mainly interested in underground formations may prefer Prometheus without feeling it has missed an essential second cave.

Visiting both can make sense for enthusiasts with enough time. It is less compelling if you are trying to add variety to a short holiday. Look at the full week's itinerary: a city morning, a forest visit and a canyon day may suit your group better than repeating similar experiences.

If a canyon is the preferred alternative, the separate [Martvili Canyon guide](/blog/martvili-canyon-tickets-boat-visit-guide) covers that choice. TripMate's bookable [Canyons Tour in Imereti Region](/tours/canyons-tour-in-imereti-region) departs from Tbilisi and visits Martvili, Okatse and Kinchkha. Its published itinerary does not include Sataplia; compare it as a different outing.

## Visiting in November: choose for the whole experience

In late autumn, decide whether you would still enjoy the tracks, cave and woodland if the panorama were limited by weather. If the answer is yes, Sataplia remains a useful candidate. If your only priority is the glass viewpoint, wait for suitable conditions and confirm access first.

Pack for both outdoor paths and a cool cave. Take water, shoes with grip and a layer you can easily put on or remove. Keep the day's optional stops flexible, particularly when travelling with children or older relatives.

The best Sataplia visit gives each part enough attention: understanding the footprints, looking at the cave formations and spending time in the forest. A modest plan leaves you with more of that experience than a long list of rushed stops.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much are Sataplia Nature Reserve tickets?

APA lists 30 GEL for foreign adults and 15 GEL for Georgian citizens and residents. Reduced child categories are shown above; confirm eligibility and bring identification.

### Are the dinosaur footprints at Sataplia real?

The reserve protects preserved dinosaur tracks. The model dinosaurs elsewhere along the visitor route are displays, so distinguish those models from the footprints when explaining the visit to children.

### Can you use the Sataplia glass platform in rain?

The official visitor rules prohibit platform access during rain or snow. Check the status before travelling if that feature is the reason for your visit.

### How long do you need at Sataplia?

We suggest two to three hours at the reserve for a relaxed visit, plus transport. Families may want extra time for questions, breaks and the outdoor sections.

### Is Sataplia better than Prometheus Cave for children?

Sataplia is our choice for children interested in dinosaur tracks and a mix of activities. Prometheus can suit a family more focused on the underground experience. Choose by interests and walking comfort.
